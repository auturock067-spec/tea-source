## Product decision and buyer jobs

Primary audience: small US specialty tea brands, tea shops, cafes and first-time commercial importers. Secondary audiences: UK, Canada, Australia and EU buyers. V0.1 should help a buyer select a category, understand origin/specification questions, plan samples and testing, and generate an actionable RFQ. It should not present an inventory catalog or accept orders.

**Research inference, not interview evidence:** seller pages repeatedly emphasize small quantities, samples, traceability and landed costs. These signals support four initial buyer problems: (1) tying up capital in poorly matched minimum orders; (2) uncertainty about sample-to-bulk consistency; (3) comparing incomplete quotes; (4) navigating import and batch-document requirements. No customer interviews or representative market-size study were conducted. Validate these hypotheses with future buyer conversations.

Recommended positioning: **“A clearer starting point for sourcing Chinese tea.”** Explain sourcing decisions; do not imply that PENG has an established supplier network or a guaranteed procurement service.

## Tea Library: six practical sourcing categories

**Taxonomy caveat:** the requested six website categories are merchandising/sourcing groups. The traditional Chinese six-category processing classification includes **yellow tea** and does not count jasmine as a primary processing category. [UNESCO](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/traditional-tea-processing-techniques-and-associated-social-practices-in-china-01884) lists green, yellow, dark, white, oolong and black, and describes flower-scented teas as reprocessed products. Label the website **“Six sourcing categories”**, not “the six traditional types.”

| Website category | Representative sourcing origins/examples | Questions for a buying brief |
|---|---|---|
| Green / 绿茶 | Zhejiang Longjing; Anhui Huangshan Maofeng | Exact producing area and cultivar, harvest date, leaf grade, heat fixation, freshness/storage, lot and residue screen. |
| Black / 红茶 | Anhui Qimen/Keemun; Fujian Zhengshan Xiaozhong | Leaf grade and cut, cultivar, oxidation/drying, smoked or unsmoked, brew application, harvest and lot. |
| Oolong / 乌龙茶 | Fujian Wuyi rock tea and Anxi Tieguanyin | Cultivar, named origin evidence, oxidation/roast and roast date, fragrance profile, packing, sample-to-lot match. |
| White / 白茶 | Fujian Fuding and Zhenghe | Bud/leaf standard, harvest and processing year, storage history, pressed/loose form, authenticity evidence for age claims. |
| Pu-erh & dark / 普洱与黑茶 | Yunnan Pu-erh (raw/sheng or ripe/shou); Hunan Anhua dark tea | Specify raw versus ripe, production and pressing dates, storage conditions, lot traceability, loose/pressed format. Do not describe all Pu-erh as identically post-fermented. |
| Jasmine / 茉莉花茶 | Fujian Fuzhou; Guangxi Hengzhou (historically Hengxian) | Base tea type and origin, flower scenting versus added flavor, scenting process, ingredients, leaf shape, harvest and lot. |

## Practical procurement guidance (editorial recommendations)

1. **Define the use:** cafe brewing, retail loose leaf, sachets or ingredient production; destination, target cup profile, leaf grade and pack format.
2. **Ask for separate minimums:** per tea, per lot, mixed shipment and custom packaging. PENG has no verified numeric MOQ. Any quantity shown in an example must be labeled illustrative, never an offer.
3. **Evaluate a coded sample:** record lot/sample code, harvest and processing dates, origin, cultivar if known, water/leaf ratio, temperature/time, sensory notes and acceptance criteria. Agree whether the sample represents the reserved commercial lot, and obtain written reapproval for substitutions.
4. **Plan evidence before paying:** seller identity; specifications; ingredient/allergen details; lot traceability; current relevant certification with scope; destination-appropriate laboratory results with batch code, analytes, methods, detection limits and sample dates. A generic “tested” badge or unrelated report is insufficient. Testing scope should follow the importer’s hazard assessment, not a universal invented panel.
5. **Compare complete quotes:** currency, grade, quantity, pack size, price validity, Incoterm and named place, freight, insurance, duties/taxes, importer responsibility, payment terms, lead time, substitution and rejection rules. A delivered price does not automatically settle regulatory responsibilities.
6. **Approve packaging:** food-contact suitability, moisture/oxygen/light barrier, closures, net weight, lot/date coding, carton protection, destination label artwork and claims. Confirm shelf life and storage with supporting evidence; do not claim indefinite shelf life for aged teas.

## US compliance: concise source-backed website copy

**US import readiness.** Imported tea must meet applicable food-safety and labeling requirements. Identify the importer and responsibilities before shipping. Relevant facilities may need FDA registration, and covered shipments need Prior Notice. FDA does not pre-approve ordinary food importers or tea products; registration is not a product-quality endorsement. [FDA importing food overview](https://www.fda.gov/importing-food-products-united-states).

**FSVP.** Where the rule applies, the FSVP importer must maintain a risk-based foreign-supplier verification program. A seller’s certificate or one test report is not a substitute for the importer’s full program; exemptions and modified requirements depend on the actual circumstances. [FDA FSVP final rule](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-safety-modernization-act-fsma/fsma-final-rule-foreign-supplier-verification-programs-fsvp-importers-food-humans-and-animals), [FDA final guidance](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/guidance-industry-foreign-supplier-verification-programs-importers-food-humans-and-animals).

**Samples still need planning.** Commercial tasting/evaluation samples are not automatically exempt from import rules merely because they are free or small. Check the actual Prior Notice and FSVP applicability before shipment. [FDA Prior Notice guidance](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/guidance-industry-what-you-need-know-about-prior-notice-imported-food-shipments).

**Label review.** Confirm identity, net quantity, ingredients/allergens where applicable, responsible business details, nutrition-label applicability and permissible claims. Some plain teas may qualify for a nutrition-label exemption; do not extend that to all flavored teas, blends or products carrying claims. [FDA labeling guidance index](https://www.fda.gov/food/guidance-documents-regulatory-information-topic-food-and-dietary-supplements/labeling-nutrition-guidance-documents-regulatory-information), [FDA nutrition-label compliance guide](https://www.fda.gov/media/134505/download). Avoid unsupported health, organic or “FDA approved” claims. Country-of-origin marking and customs matters also require separate review with the broker.

**Current change to track:** FDA’s [Prior Notice page](https://www.fda.gov/industry/fda-import-process/prior-notice-imported-foods) states that an international-mail service/tracking requirement was effective October 1, 2026, with enforcement discretion delaying expected submission of those two details until May 1, 2027. Keep this as a dated maintenance note rather than hardcoding a simplified deadline in evergreen consumer copy.

## Other destination checkpoints

- **EU:** select the correct tea commodity and pesticide in the EU MRL database, and check current limits and effective dates. A generic “EU compliant” claim without lot-specific evidence and scope is not enough. Organic certification does not replace residue or other safety requirements. [European Commission MRL overview](https://food.ec.europa.eu/plants/pesticides/maximum-residue-levels_en). Other labeling, contaminants and border controls also need destination-specific review; this page is not an exhaustive import checklist.
- **UK / Great Britain:** do not assume EU and GB requirements are identical. Chinese tea is subject to pesticide-related HRFNAO restrictions; check the current commodity/country listing, required documents, pre-notification and designated border route before shipping. Northern Ireland has separate guidance. [FSA import hub](https://www.food.gov.uk/business-guidance/imports-exports), [FSA HRFNAO guidance](https://www.food.gov.uk/print/pdf/node/168), [FSA border notification explanation](https://www.food.gov.uk/our-work/border-notifications). The current Chinese-tea restriction is also acknowledged in the [FSA Committee on Toxicity January 2026 summary](https://cot.food.gov.uk/Summary%20-%20green%20and%20black%20tea%20in%20the%20maternal%20diet). No check percentage is asserted here because annexes change.
- **Canada:** most commercial food importers require an SFC licence; confirm the specific product/activity and applicable exceptions, preventive controls, traceability and Canadian labeling requirements. [CFIA importing food overview](https://inspection.canada.ca/en/food-safety-industry/toolkit-food-businesses/importing-food). Unprocessed tea leaves for further preparation may have different licensing treatment; do not claim every tea shipment has the same rule. [CFIA licensed activities](https://inspection.canada.ca/en/food-licences/food-business-activities).
- **Australia:** use BICON for the exact ingredients, processing, use and shipment scenario, then check food standards. Plain Camellia sinensis and herbal/flower blends can follow different conditions; a personal baggage allowance is not commercial import permission. [DAFF commercial plant-food guidance](https://www.agriculture.gov.au/import/goods/plant-products/importing-plant-products-for-human-consumption), [DAFF BICON overview](https://www.agriculture.gov.au/import/online-services/bicon).

All compliance sources accessed 2026-10-26. These are orientation checkpoints. Buyers should verify current rules for their exact product and obtain professional advice where needed. PENG is not representing itself as an importer, legal adviser, laboratory or certification body.

## Naming and trademark preliminary screen

- **Observed conflict:** TeaSource actively sells tea and operates wholesale services at teasource.com. Related goods/channels make public use of the exact TeaSource name a meaningful confusion risk. This is observed commercial use, not a finding about registration ownership/status.
- **Interim decision:** keep internal repository `tea-source`; public-facing site uses **PENG Tea Sourcing**. No affiliation with TeaSource is claimed. Keep name, site URL and contact in one configuration module to support renaming.
- **Scope limit:** an exact-phrase web query for “PENG Tea Sourcing” did not surface an identifiable exact match in returned results; this does **not** establish availability. No complete live-register, state, common-law, phonetic, translated-name, UKIPO/EUIPO/CIPO/IP Australia or WIPO clearance was completed. Do not describe the provisional name as cleared or registered.
- [USPTO likelihood of confusion](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search/likelihood-confusion), [USPTO federal trademark searching](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search/federal-trademark-searching), [USPTO comprehensive clearance](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search/comprehensive-clearance-search-similar-trademarks): similar names and related goods can conflict; federal searching alone is not comprehensive. Accessed 2026-10-26.
- Recommend formal destination-market clearance before packaging investment, paid acquisition, trademark filing or significant commercial expansion. This does not justify inventing a legal conclusion or holding educational V0.1 development indefinitely.

## RFQ content and truthful status copy

Fields: tea category; target market; intended use; grade/cultivar/origin preference; quantity and units; sample requirement; packaging format/net weight/artwork; quality/tests/documents; delivery location and timing; Incoterm preference; buyer email; optional company and additional notes.

Safe completion message: **“Your sourcing brief is ready. Nothing has been sent. Download or copy it, then send it to your chosen supplier.”**

中文：**“采购需求书已生成，尚未发送。请下载或复制后，自行发给所选供应商。”**

If there is no real receiving address, do not invent one and do not label the button “Submit RFQ.” A mailto draft should require a recipient supplied by the visitor and explain that the visitor must review and send it in their own email application. Do not collect or retain form content server-side in the static V0.1.
