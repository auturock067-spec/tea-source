## Search intent

Primary themes: Chinese tea sourcing, small-batch tea procurement, Chinese tea categories, tea sourcing checklist and tea RFQ template. Category pages target useful specific topics such as green tea sourcing questions, Wuyi oolong specifications, white tea harvest/storage and raw versus ripe Pu-erh. Use natural headings and descriptions; avoid keyword repetition or ranking promises.

Use descriptive internal links between category, region, guide, compliance and RFQ. Keep English and Chinese on stable separate routes with canonical/alternate language metadata. Publish sitemap and robots; check them after production hostname confirmation. No fabricated review, price, stock or organization markup. No search-engine indexing outcome is guaranteed by a successful deployment.

## Editorial rules

- Use “six sourcing categories.” The traditional six processing categories include yellow; jasmine is a scented/reprocessed tea, and Pu-erh raw/ripe processing differs.
- Explain representative origins without implying every item bearing a name is authenticated. Ask for specific origin, cultivar, harvest/process dates and lot evidence.
- Do not publish prices, universal MOQ ranges, sample availability promises, delivery guarantees, supplier relationships or service response times without operational evidence.
- Separate factual source statements from buying recommendations. Laboratory panels depend on the actual food/market/hazard assessment; a generic certificate does not prove batch compliance.
- Link official rules close to claims. State reviewed date and scope; no legal-advice or customs-clearance guarantee.
- Avoid health/treatment claims, unsupported organic assertions, “FDA approved tea,” customer counts and invented testimonials.
- Plain-text RFQ results are requests for information, not quotes, orders or a purchase commitment. No receiving inbox is connected.

## Sources and asset rights

Prefer FDA, European Commission, FSA, CFIA, DAFF/BICON, UNESCO, CNIPA and government origin references. First-party competitor descriptions are research observations, not PENG claims. Paraphrase facts, link sources and record access dates. External logos, competitor images and government photos are not reused. Site illustrations are original repository-native SVGs. Retain provenance records for any future external asset and satisfy its licence before publishing.

## Bilingual review checklist

English and Chinese must agree on quantities/units, form constraints, market responsibilities, sample caveats, absence of transmission, and claim strength. Translate “black tea” as 红茶 and distinguish “dark tea” 黑茶. Keep real place names understandable in English and Chinese. Switching languages should reach the equivalent page; user-entered RFQ data is not promised to survive navigation.

## Ownership and cadence

Project owner approves commercial claims and contact details. Maintainer reviews official links/regulatory changes monthly, with particular attention to US Prior Notice, EU pesticide limits and GB Chinese-tea controls; review both languages and sources quarterly. Update config review date only after an actual review. Record material changes in Git and rerun affected page/flow tests. Recheck provisional naming before paid marketing, packaging investment or service expansion.

Future content should follow actual buyer questions and verified operational evidence. Supplier profiles, available lots, numeric offers, inbox submission and transaction features remain outside this release until their evidence and process exist.
